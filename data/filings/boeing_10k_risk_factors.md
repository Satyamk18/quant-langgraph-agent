# The Boeing Company (BA) — Annual Report (Form 10-K)
## Item 1A. Risk Factors

### Debt Obligations, Refinancing Risks, and Liquidity Constraints
As of December 31, our consolidated debt totaled approximately $48.4 billion, compared to cash and short-term investments of $19.3 billion. Our substantial level of debt could have significant consequences on our financial flexibility and operational performance:
- It limits our ability to obtain additional financing on favorable terms for working capital, capital expenditures, or debt service requirements.
- A downgrade in our credit ratings by major credit rating agencies (Standard & Poor's, Moody's, or Fitch) would increase borrowing costs and reduce access to commercial paper markets.
- A substantial portion of our operating cash flows must be dedicated to payment of principal and interest on existing debt, reducing funds available for commercial aircraft research, development (such as the 777X program), and capital investment.
- Incurrence of additional debt to finance operational shortfalls or production slowdowns could restrict our compliance with existing financial covenants.

### Operational Risks: Commercial Aircraft Production, Quality Oversight, and FAA Directives
We face ongoing regulatory oversight and operational challenges regarding the 737 program and 777X certification:
- Following the January 2024 door-plug incident on a 737-9 aircraft, the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) halted production rate expansion for the 737 MAX until comprehensive quality management milestones are satisfied.
- Increased quality inspections at our Renton factory and supplier Spirit AeroSystems have led to delivery delays, increased inventory holding costs, and contractual customer compensation obligations.
- Delays in the certification schedule for the 777-9 and 737-7/737-10 variants expose the company to potential airline order cancellations and financial penalties.

## Item 7. Management’s Discussion and Analysis (MD&A)
### Free Cash Flow and Capital Allocation
Free cash flow has been negatively impacted by inventory buildup and delayed commercial aircraft deliveries. Fixed-price development contracts within the Boeing Defense, Space & Security (BDS) segment (notably KC-46A Tanker and Commercial Crew) have continued to record operating losses due to engineering rework and inflationary supply chain pressure.
